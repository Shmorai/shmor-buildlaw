JCT vs NEC: which construction contract is right for you?

JCT and NEC are the UK’s two leading families of construction contract. Both comply with the Construction Act and both work well, but they ask very different things of the people running them.

By the BuildLaw editorial team · 3 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Amina Atar on Unsplash

Key differences

JCTNEC4
StyleTraditional legal draftingPlain, present-tense language
ManagementContract administrator certifies; fewer process stepsActive management: early warnings, accepted programme, strict response times
ChangeVariations valued under valuation rules; time and money assessed separatelyCompensation events assessed for time and money together, using quotations
Time barsNotice “forthwith” for delay; fewer strict time bars8-week time bar for notifying compensation events
Pricing optionsDifferent forms for different routesMain Options A–F within one contract
Typical usersPrivate-sector buildingPublic sector and infrastructure, increasingly building

Which suits you?

  • Choose NEC if both sides have the people and systems to run its processes, you want collaborative risk management and you value a live programme.
  • Choose JCT for many traditional building projects, where the team and supply chain know it well and you prefer a lighter administrative load.

Frequently asked questions

Is NEC more collaborative than JCT?
NEC builds collaboration into its processes, with a duty to act in a spirit of mutual trust and co-operation. JCT has collaborative provisions too, and a Collaborative Contract form. How collaborative a project is depends mostly on the people.
