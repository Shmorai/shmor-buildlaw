URS v BDW: why defects from decades ago can still cost you

In May 2025 the Supreme Court gave its first major decision on the Building Safety Act 2022’s extended time limits. The result matters to anyone who designs or builds homes in England and Wales.

By the BuildLaw editorial team · 1 October 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: Robin the Bird on Unsplash

The background

BDW, a housebuilder, engaged URS as structural engineer for two high-rise residential developments. After selling the flats, BDW discovered serious structural design defects and paid for remedial work, though it no longer owned the buildings and hadn’t been sued by the owners. It claimed the cost from URS in negligence and, after the Building Safety Act 2022 came into force, under the Defective Premises Act 1972.

What the Supreme Court decided

On 21 May 2025 the Supreme Court dismissed URS’s appeal on all grounds ([2025] UKSC 21). In summary:

  • BDW’s repair costs weren’t irrecoverable simply because it chose to pay them; whether they were recoverable depended on ordinary principles such as causation and mitigation.
  • The Building Safety Act’s retrospective extension of the Defective Premises Act limitation period, from 6 years to 30 years for claims that arose before 28 June 2022, could benefit BDW’s claims against URS.
  • A developer like BDW could itself be owed duties under section 1 of the Defective Premises Act.
  • The court also considered when a party can bring a contribution claim against another party liable for the same damage.

What it means

  • Designers and contractors on residential projects in England and Wales can face claims about work completed decades ago. Keep project records, and check how long your insurance and record-keeping cover.
  • Developers who put right defects can, in principle, seek to recover the cost from those responsible, even after selling.
  • Everyone should take the Defective Premises Act seriously: it can’t be excluded by contract.

Frequently asked questions

Does the 30-year limit apply to all construction defects?
No. The retrospective 30-year period applies to claims under section 1 of the Defective Premises Act 1972 concerning dwellings. Ordinary contract claims still have 6- or 12-year limits.
