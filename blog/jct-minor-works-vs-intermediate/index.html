JCT Minor Works vs Intermediate: which should you use?

Minor Works and Intermediate are JCT’s two most popular forms for small and medium projects. They look similar, but suit different jobs. Here’s how to choose.

By the BuildLaw editorial team · 4 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Annika Wischnewsky on Unsplash

At a glance

Minor Works (MW / MWD)Intermediate (IC / ICD)
Best forSimple, small worksMedium projects of moderate complexity
DesignClient’s design; MWD adds contractor design of specified partsClient’s design; ICD adds a contractor-designed portion
DetailShort, simple provisionsFuller provisions, for example named specialists, sectional completion and more detailed insurance and termination clauses
AdministrationArchitect/contract administratorArchitect/contract administrator

JCT’s own guidance describes each form’s intended use, and there’s no fixed financial limit for either. See the full JCT family.

When Minor Works is enough

  • The work is simple and fully designed before tender.
  • The period is short and there are no complex services installations.
  • There’s no need for sections, named subcontractors or detailed control procedures.

When to choose Intermediate (or more)

  • The project involves several trades and some complexity, but not enough for the Standard Building Contract or NEC.
  • You want to name specialists, complete in sections or have more detailed provisions.
  • Step up to the Standard Building Contract for larger or complex projects, or where bills of quantities are used.

For a homeowner, use JCT’s homeowner contracts instead: see homeowners and builders.

General information, not legal advice. What applies to you depends on your contract, the facts and where the work is, and the law changes. Check the sources below and take advice from a qualified professional before acting on anything that matters.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a value limit for JCT Minor Works?
JCT doesn’t set a fixed limit; it describes the type of project each form suits. Complexity and risk matter more than the contract sum.
