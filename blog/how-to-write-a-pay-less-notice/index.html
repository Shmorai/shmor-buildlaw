How to write a valid pay less notice

A pay less notice is the payer’s only way to pay less than the notified sum under a construction contract. Get the timing or content wrong and you may have to pay the full amount first. Here’s how to get it right.

By the BuildLaw editorial team · 14 October 2026 · 2 min read

On this page

  1. What the law requires
  2. A checklist for a valid notice
  3. Common mistakes
  4. Example wording

What the law requires

Under section 111 of the Housing Grants, Construction and Regeneration Act 1996, a payer who intends to pay less than the notified sum must give a notice stating the sum it considers due on the date of the notice and the basis on which that sum is calculated. The notice must be given no later than the prescribed period before the final date for payment: whatever the contract says or, under the Scheme, 7 days. See how the payment rules work.

A checklist for a valid notice

  1. Label it clearly as a pay less notice and refer to the contract and the payment cycle.
  2. State a sum. A notice that only lists deductions, without the sum considered due, risks being invalid.
  3. Show the basis of calculation: which items are valued lower and why, and any set-off (for example defects or liquidated damages), with enough detail for the payee to understand it.
  4. Serve it in time, counting days as the Construction Act requires (bank holidays don’t count). Our free payment deadline calculator on the payments guide works this out.
  5. Serve it the right way: to the address, person and by the method the contract’s notice clause requires. Keep proof.
  6. Serve it from the right person: the payer, or a person the contract allows to give notices on its behalf.

Common mistakes

  • Serving the notice a day late, often by forgetting a bank holiday or the contract’s own notice rules.
  • Treating a payment notice as a pay less notice without saying so. A single notice can sometimes do both jobs, but only if it clearly meets both sets of requirements and is served in time.
  • Relying on an email when the contract requires post or hand delivery.
  • Withholding for liquidated damages without the separate notices the contract requires for deducting them.

If the notice is invalid, the payer must usually pay the notified sum and then argue about value afterwards, as the Court of Appeal confirmed in S&T v Grove. Read our smash-and-grab adjudication explainer.

Frequently asked questions

Can a pay less notice be served by email?
Only if the contract allows notices by email, or the parties have agreed it. JCT 2024 lets the parties agree electronic notices. Otherwise use the method in the notice clause.
Can I serve a pay less notice for zero?
Yes. If the payer genuinely considers nothing is due, the notice can state a sum of zero, but it must still show the basis of calculation.
