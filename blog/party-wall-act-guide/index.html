The Party Wall Act: a guide for owners and builders

Extensions, loft conversions and basements often affect a shared wall or a neighbour’s foundations. In England and Wales, the Party Wall etc. Act 1996 sets out a notice process that must be followed before work starts.

By the BuildLaw editorial team · 24 September 2026 · 2 min read

On this page

  1. Which works need notice
  2. The 14-day rule
  3. Surveyors and awards
  4. For builders

Which works need notice

  • Building a new wall on the boundary (line of junction): at least one month’s notice (section 1).
  • Work to an existing party wall or party structure, such as cutting in beams, raising or underpinning it: a party structure notice at least two months before work starts (section 3).
  • Excavating near a neighbour’s building: within 3 metres and deeper than their foundations, or within 6 metres where the excavation would cut a 45-degree line from their foundations, at least one month’s notice (section 6).

The Act applies in England and Wales. Scotland and Northern Ireland have different rules.

The 14-day rule

The neighbour (adjoining owner) can consent in writing. If they don’t consent within 14 days of a party structure notice (or an excavation notice), a dispute is deemed to have arisen. That doesn’t mean a fight: it means surveyors are appointed.

Surveyors and awards

The owners either agree on one surveyor or each appoints their own, and the surveyors make a party wall award setting out what work can be done, how and when, and usually recording the condition of the neighbour’s property. The building owner normally pays the reasonable surveyors’ costs. An award can be appealed to the county court within 14 days.

For builders

Party wall notices are usually the owner’s responsibility, but contracts should say who deals with them, and starting work before the process is complete can cause delays and claims. Add it to your homeowner contract checklist and see building regulations dutyholders.

Frequently asked questions

Is a party wall agreement the same as planning permission?
No. Party wall procedures, planning permission and building regulations approval are separate. You may need all three.
What if my neighbour ignores the notice?
If they don’t respond within 14 days, a dispute is deemed to have arisen. If they then fail to appoint a surveyor after a further notice, the building owner can appoint one on their behalf.
