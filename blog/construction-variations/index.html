Construction variations: instructing, pricing and getting paid

Change is normal on building projects. Disputes come from changes that were never properly instructed, priced or recorded. Here’s how variations work under UK contracts.

By the BuildLaw editorial team · 6 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Daniel McCullough on Unsplash

On this page

  1. What counts as a variation
  2. Instruct it properly
  3. How variations are valued
  4. Homeowners and extras

What counts as a variation

Under JCT, a variation (in Design and Build, a “change”) is a change to the design, quality or quantity of the works, or to obligations or restrictions such as access, working hours or sequence. Under NEC4, an instruction changing the Scope is a compensation event. Work the contractor was always required to do isn’t a variation, so read the specification and contract terms first.

Instruct it properly

  • Only the person named in the contract (architect/contract administrator, employer’s agent or project manager) can instruct variations.
  • Instructions should be in writing. If you receive one orally, confirm it in writing straight away.
  • Ask for a quotation or agree a price before work starts where possible.

How variations are valued

JCT uses valuation rules: contract rates where the work is similar, adjusted rates where conditions differ, then fair rates, or daywork where appropriate, unless a quotation was accepted. NEC4 assesses compensation events by their effect on Defined Cost plus the fee, using quotations. Variations can also affect time: claim an extension of time if they delay completion.

Homeowners and extras

On home projects, unagreed “extras” are a top cause of disputes. Agree every change in writing, with a price, before it’s done. See homeowners and builders.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to carry out a variation I disagree with?
Under most standard forms, the contractor must comply with valid instructions, though it may object to some on reasonable grounds (for example, under JCT, certain changes to access or working conditions). Comply, record your objection and claim time and money.
