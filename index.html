    Key rules at a glance

    Some of the rules that matter most on UK building projects. Each one links to a full guide with the conditions and exceptions.

    Payment rules in full →
    Payment

    Payment notice: 5 days

    The payer must say what it will pay, and how that sum is worked out, no later than 5 days after each payment due date.

    Payment notices →    Payment

    Pay less before the deadline

    To pay less than the notified sum, the payer must serve a pay less notice in time. Under the Scheme, that’s at least 7 days before the final date for payment.

    Pay less notices →    Payment

    Suspend on 7 days’ notice

    If the notified sum isn’t paid by the final date, the payee can suspend work after giving at least 7 days’ notice of intention to suspend.

    Right to suspend →    Disputes

    Adjudicate at any time

    Either party to a construction contract can refer a dispute to adjudication at any time. The decision is due within 28 days of referral, unless extended.

    How adjudication works →    Late payment

    Statutory interest

    On business-to-business debts, the Late Payment Act lets you claim interest at 8% above the Bank of England base rate, unless the contract has a substantial remedy.

    Late payment interest →    Payment

    No “pay when paid”

    “Pay when paid” clauses are generally ineffective under the Construction Act, except where a party further up the chain is insolvent.

    Unfair payment terms →    Disputes

    Courts can order mediation

    Since October 2024 the Civil Procedure Rules expressly allow courts in England and Wales to order parties to engage in ADR, such as mediation.

    What changed →    Defects

    30 years for some defects

    The Building Safety Act 2022 extended the time limit for Defective Premises Act claims about dwellings to 30 years for claims that arose before 28 June 2022, and 15 years for later ones.

    Construction law in plain English

    Building work in the UK runs on contracts, and on a set of statutory rules that sit underneath them. The Housing Grants, Construction and Regeneration Act 1996 (the “Construction Act”) gives most parties to construction contracts the right to be paid in stages, to know what they’ll be paid and why, to suspend work for non-payment and to refer disputes to adjudication at any time. Standard forms such as JCT and NEC then set out who does what, when, for how much and what happens when things change.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is BuildLaw a law firm?
    No. BuildLaw is a free construction law information website. We don’t give legal advice or represent anyone. If you need advice, our services page explains how we can introduce you to independent, qualified professionals, who work under their own terms.
    Does BuildLaw cover Scotland and Northern Ireland?
    Yes. Our guides are written mainly for England and Wales, and we flag where Scotland or Northern Ireland differ. The Construction Act applies in England, Wales and Scotland, with separate Scheme regulations in Scotland, and Northern Ireland has its own Construction Contracts (Northern Ireland) Order 1997.
    Do the payment rules apply to work on my house?
    Mostly not. The Construction Act’s payment and adjudication rules don’t apply to contracts with a residential occupier for work on the home they live in, unless the contract itself includes them (some homeowner contracts do). Homeowners are protected by consumer law instead. See homeowners and builders.
    Can I use your example notices and letters?
    Yes, they’re free to adapt. They’re examples, not templates guaranteed for your situation: always check them against the payment and notice clauses in your own contract, which may set different deadlines or addresses for notices.
